# Final submission checklist

Status: preparation checklist. Unchecked items are requirements to verify, not claims that the work is missing from every local artifact. Complete promptly as evidence becomes available; October 25 is a deadline, not a target date to wait for.

## Assignment essentials

- [ ] A short legend explains the original system in ordinary language: time, layers, brightness, noise-like character, entrances/releases, silence, and overlap.
- [ ] Two actual applied scores are listening-reviewed, readable, and explicitly tied to the reference excerpts. Generated drafts are labeled honestly until reviewed.
- [ ] The same symbol meanings apply to both pieces. Any calibration or manual adjustment is disclosed.
- [ ] Include artist/title, usable reference link, and exact excerpt/version. For Choice, explain the 20-second boundary belongs to the linked drum/bass adaptation.
- [ ] Export both scores in a format the TA can view without running software. Start with `docs/scores/trumpet.html` and `choice.html` plus their SVGs; inspect the actual exported/printed result for legibility and complete tables.
- [ ] Include the approximately 1,000-word reflection in the student's voice, with actual listening experiences and lessons. `REFLECTION_DRAFT.md` is scaffolding, not a finished essay.

## Evidence and honesty

- [ ] Review suggested gesture boundaries and properties by listening; retain before/after files and a correction log.
- [ ] Record why the final layer representation was chosen. Compare mixture, HPSS, and Demucs on common times rather than claiming separation quality from successful execution.
- [ ] If reporting feedback, include actual count, task wording, order, responses, and disagreements. Do not imply musician validation if it has not happened.
- [ ] Clearly distinguish original audio, separated stems, score synthesis, neutral baseline, and deliberately edited synthesis.
- [ ] Explain that synthesis consumes the complete stored score; the ribbon alone omits supporting parameters. Keep the full gesture table with the score when practical.
- [ ] Do not present automated descriptors as direct measurements of perceptual qualities or sound labels as proof that an instrument is present.

## Optional playable demonstration and reproducibility

- [ ] Open both `examples/*.json` files in a clean session; inspect, edit, undo, save, reopen, and play. Verify actual audio/score timing and record listening observations.
- [ ] Compare matched A/B/C/D files from `outputs/studies/`; retain raw files and the report's gain/target/edit record. Equal RMS is a control, not a guarantee of equal perceived loudness.
- [ ] Verify source/model provenance and the current documented setup/check commands. Preserve exact version information for the submitted build.
- [ ] Inspect final static pages at ordinary display/print size, especially dense Choice marks. Ensure the full table or an enlarged view resolves hidden short-event labels.
- [ ] Include attribution, adaptation notices, and applicable licenses with shared source-derived material; keep large recordings/models out of ordinary Git.
- [ ] Open the uploaded submission as a reader would and confirm all required files and reference links are available.

The code and playback are supporting artifacts. A working app does not replace the two readable applied scores and the reflection required by the assignment.
